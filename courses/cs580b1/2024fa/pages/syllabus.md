### Description

The objective of this course is to provide students with a working knowledge of the state of research and practice on trustworthy machine learning. Students will learn about the failure modes of state-of-the-art machine learning models and AI-enabled systems. They will learn about techniques for discovering and mitigating these vulnerabilities of machine learning models, specially deep neural networks (DNNs). They will learn why DNNs are referred to as black boxes and about approaches for "opening" these black boxes to understand their internal behaviors. Along the way, they will get a review of DNN basics as well as a lightweight introduction (i) to formal methods for rigorously analyzing software systems, (ii) to Large Language Models (LLMs), and (iii) to learning-enabled cyber-physical systems. Students will also learn how to read, review, and present research papers.

### Topics

The topics covered in this course are listed below (subject to change, not in exact order, as time permits):

* Review of DNN Basics
* Introduction to Formal Methods
* Robustness
* Interpretability
* Fairness
* Privacy
* Calibration
* Introduction to LLMs
* LLM Jailbreaking, Alignment, and Memorization
* Safety of Learning-enabled Cyber-physical Systems

### Audience and Prerequisites

This course is geared towards graduate students in Computer Science. There are no official prerequisites but some background in machine learning and prior exposure to mathematical proofs will be helpful.

### Paper reviews

We will generally aim to discuss one research paper every week. In a typical week, we will learn about a new topic on Tuesday and then discuss a research paper on this topic on Thursday. Students will be required to submit a review for every paper discussed in class (roughly about 10-12 papers over the semester). Paper reviews need to be turned in electronically via Canvas by 11:59 pm on the due date (which will always be the day before a paper will be discussed in class). All the reviews should be written **individually** but you are free to discuss with your classmates before you start writing the review. The reviews should be typeset using [LaTeX](https://www.latex-project.org/). You can either install LaTeX locally or access it in a web browser via [Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/).


**Policy:** Upto two reviews can be submitted 48 hours late without any penalty. Any additional late submission will receive no credit. Penalties for assignments and projects are as follows:

Additional late submissions may be allowed without penalty only with prior permission on a case-by-case basis. If you cannot finish the work by the deadline, contact the instructor as soon as possible. Extensions will be granted on a case-by-case basis and are more likely when permission is sought in advance, for reasons which are unexpected and beyond your control, and which involve only a short extension. The instructor reserves the right to assign a score penalty to the late work, depending on the circumstances and degree of lateness.

### Term project

The term project (in teams of up to three) involves some independent research on topics relevant to the course. This could involve performing a detailed literature survey on a specific topic, replicating the results in a research paper, applying the tools and techniques presented in the class to your own ML models,  or proposing and evaluating a new idea. Submissions related to the term project will involve a proposal, a mid-point update, and a final report. The term-project will start around the halfway point of the semester. Students will present the projects in the last week of lectures.

**Policy:** *Late submissions are not allowed for any term project deliverable.*

### Paper presentations
Students (in teams of two or three) will be required to present two research papers during the semester. The presentation will be approximately 20-30 minuntes long. A typical format for such a class would be as follows:

* Presenters summarizes the research paper being discussed and puts forth points of discussion in the first 30 minutes.
* Instructor then leads a discussion on the paper for the remainder of the class.

### Class participation

Students must attend lectures and actively participate in class discussions. Additionally, students should participate in the forum discussions on MS Teams.
